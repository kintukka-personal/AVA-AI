# **Comprehensive Summary of the AVA Introduction Deck**

## **Overview**

The document introduces **AVA (Atlas Virtual Assistant)** as a **governed AI navigation tool** designed to help users working in **D365 Finance & Operations** quickly locate and access **approved procedure guidance**. The overall positioning of AVA is very deliberate: it is not presented as a replacement for training, nor as a replacement for support, but as a **controlled navigation layer** that helps users find the correct information faster and with more confidence.

The tone and structure of the presentation suggest that AVA has been designed to address a very specific operational problem within the organisation: users spending too much time searching through documentation, raising routine “how do I” queries, or relying on inconsistent informal guidance when trying to complete D365 tasks. The deck frames AVA as a solution to this problem by giving users near-instant access to **approved, governed, source-based procedural content**.

At its core, the presentation communicates three things:

1.  **What AVA is**

2.  **How AVA works**

3.  **Why AVA matters**

It also includes user guidance on how to access and use the tool, an example of expected behaviour, and a feedback/reporting mechanism to support pilot adoption and continuous improvement.

# **Detailed Summary by Theme**

## **1. What AVA is**

AVA is described as the **Atlas Virtual Assistant**, a **governed WTG documentation assistant**. The deck makes it clear that the assistant’s primary role is to:

- instantly deliver **approved D365 procedures**

- direct users to the **correct source documentation**

- reduce time spent searching for relevant guidance

- support users in navigating procedure guides efficiently

The presentation strongly positions AVA as a **documentation retrieval and navigation tool**, rather than a general AI chatbot. This distinction is important for client handover because it defines the tool’s intended use and its operating boundaries.

### **Key characteristics implied by the deck:**

- It is **governed**, meaning it operates within controlled content boundaries

- It is tied specifically to **approved procedure guides**

- It provides **evidence-backed answers**

- It helps users navigate official documentation rather than inventing or improvising answers

- It is part of a broader D365 enablement ecosystem

The phrase **“Governed AI Navigation for D365 — Instantly Access Approved Procedure Guides”** is essentially the value proposition of the entire tool. It tells the client that AVA is not just AI for AI’s sake — it is AI applied to a practical enterprise need: documentation access, consistency, and controlled guidance.

## **2. How AVA is positioned within the support ecosystem**

One of the most important messages in the deck is that AVA is a **navigation layer**, not a substitute for other enablement or operational functions.

The presentation explicitly states that AVA is:

- **not a training replacement**

- **not a support replacement**

- **an aid**

This is a critical point for handover because it helps set expectations for stakeholders and end users.

### **What this means in practice:**

AVA is intended to sit between the user and the documentation library as a smart retrieval interface. Rather than replacing structured training, AVA complements training by helping users recall and locate steps after training has already occurred. Likewise, rather than replacing support teams, AVA aims to reduce low-value, repeatable navigation questions so that support resources can focus on exceptions, genuine issues, and higher-complexity queries.

This positioning reduces risk in adoption because it:

- avoids overstating the tool’s purpose

- prevents users from assuming AVA can solve everything

- reinforces governance and support boundaries

- aligns the tool to a practical, assistive use case

In other words, AVA is framed as a **productivity and consistency enabler**, not an all-purpose decision-maker.

## **3. AVA’s operating model**

The deck explains AVA through a simple four-step interaction model:

1.  **User asks a question**

2.  **AVA locates relevant information**

3.  **Approved steps are displayed**

4.  **Approved sources are cited as evidence**

This sequence is central to understanding how the tool behaves.

### **Step 1: User asks a question**

AVA begins with a natural-language user query related to D365. The user is expected to ask a business-process or system-related question, ideally with a reasonable level of specificity.

This implies that AVA is designed to support users at the point of need, particularly when they:

- are unsure how to perform a task

- need to locate the right procedure guide

- want the exact approved steps for a process

- need a source-backed answer quickly

### **Step 2: AVA locates relevant information**

AVA then searches for the most relevant approved content. The deck suggests that this retrieval process is based on existing approved D365 procedure guides and that AVA attempts to identify the best matching source material.

This indicates that AVA functions as a retrieval layer over a controlled document library rather than a generative system operating without boundaries.

### **Step 3: Approved steps are displayed**

Once a successful match is found, AVA displays the approved procedure steps. This is a highly important governance feature. The goal is not merely to point users to a document, but also to surface the relevant instructions directly where possible.

This reduces friction for users by:

- shortening the time between question and answer

- reducing the need to manually search within documents

- helping users act immediately

- keeping the response grounded in approved content

### **Step 4: Approved sources are cited as evidence**

AVA includes citations to the source documentation used in the response. This is one of the strongest trust-building mechanisms in the deck.

The citation behaviour suggests that AVA is designed to:

- show users where the answer came from

- reinforce confidence in the output

- maintain traceability

- support governance and auditability

- encourage use of approved material rather than undocumented tribal knowledge

This is especially valuable in an enterprise environment, because it means AVA is not presenting instructions as unsupported AI-generated content; it is presenting them as sourced outputs grounded in approved documentation.

## **4. AVA’s possible response pathways**

The deck outlines **two potential outcomes** when a user submits a query.

### **Outcome 1: Standard retrieval**

If the information is successfully located, AVA:

- identifies the exact procedure guide

- returns the relevant information

- cites the source

This is the ideal and intended pathway. It reflects AVA’s role as a successful documentation retrieval engine.

### **Outcome 2: Out-of-scope / unable to locate information**

If the information cannot be located, AVA redirects the user to support.

This is another important governance mechanism. Instead of guessing, hallucinating, or attempting to answer beyond its knowledge boundary, AVA is expected to recognise when it cannot confidently locate the required information and then guide the user toward the appropriate support path.

This behaviour is a strong indicator that the tool has been designed with:

- controlled boundaries

- escalation pathways

- responsible fail behaviour

- operational realism

For client handover, this is worth highlighting because it demonstrates that AVA is not intended to create risk through overreach. Its design acknowledges that not every query can or should be answered through documentation retrieval alone.

## **5. The speed and user experience promise**

The presentation repeatedly emphasises speed. One of the clearest messages is that AVA moves users **“from question to answer in seconds.”**

This matters because the tool is positioned not just as a documentation repository, but as a way to reduce the practical cost of finding information.

The implicit problem statement is:

- users lose time searching through procedure guides

- routine navigation delays slow down work

- support teams field unnecessary repetitive questions

- valuable knowledge exists, but retrieval is inefficient

AVA’s promise is to solve this by compressing the time between:

- needing an answer

- finding the right guide

- understanding the right step

- acting confidently

This is reinforced again later in the impact summary through the phrase:  
**“Turn 15-minute confusion into 15-second clarity.”**

That line captures the entire business case in one sentence. It suggests that AVA’s primary value is not that it creates new knowledge, but that it dramatically improves access to existing approved knowledge.

## **6. User access and practical usage instructions**

The deck includes a **Quick Reference Card** that explains how users should access and use AVA. This adds a practical onboarding element to the presentation.

### **The usage flow described is:**

#### **1. Open AVA**

AVA is available from the **D365 Procedure Guide SharePoint page**. Users are instructed to open the **Atlas Virtual Assistant (AVA) .agent file** at the top of the screen.

This tells us that AVA is embedded or linked within an existing documentation environment rather than existing as an entirely separate platform. That is useful for client handover because it shows AVA has been integrated into the broader documentation ecosystem.

#### **2. Ask a D365-related question**

Users can either:

- use pre-set questions at the top of the screen, or

- type their own question into the chat box

Users are also advised to be as specific as possible.

This implies the tool supports both guided and open-ended user entry, which is good for adoption:

- pre-set questions support confidence and ease of use

- free text supports real-world flexibility

The prompt to be specific also suggests that retrieval quality depends partly on query clarity, which is normal for a governed document-based assistant.

#### **3. AVA responds with strict reference to existing D365 Procedure Guides**

The Quick Reference Card explicitly states that AVA will **only provide information from D365 Procedure Guides (PGs)** and will include citations to every unique PG referenced in the response.

This reinforces the core governance model:

- no unsupported answers

- no freeform advice outside the approved content base

- clear source traceability

- strong boundaries around content use

The card also notes that AVA can both:

- help navigate users to the best suited PG

- extract the information directly if it has been successfully identified

This is important because it means AVA supports both:

- **discovery**, where the user needs help finding the right document

- **retrieval**, where the user wants the exact information surfaced immediately

#### **4. Open the related Procedure Guide if required**

AVA links the related PGs, and users can open them directly. The card notes that more than one guide may be linked and users may need to expand the section to view all linked sources.

This tells us AVA can identify multiple relevant documents where appropriate, which is helpful in cases where a process spans more than one guide or where the answer is supported across multiple references.

## **7. Example extraction behaviour**

The Quick Reference Card includes an example of successful extraction and notes that **upon a successful match (\>95)**, AVA will extract the exact required section from the approved procedure guides.

This is a significant detail.

### **What it implies:**

- there is some matching/confidence threshold in AVA’s retrieval logic

- AVA is designed to extract exact content when confidence is high enough

- the system is likely tuned to prioritise precision over overreaching

- the goal is to return the correct portion of the source material, not just a broad document suggestion

For client handover, this helps describe the maturity of the tool’s behaviour:

- it does not simply dump whole documents

- it attempts targeted retrieval

- it appears to rely on a confidence-based mechanism before surfacing extracted content

That supports both usability and governance.

## **8. Business value and impact summary**

One full slide is dedicated to the impact of AVA. This slide translates the tool from a functional asset into a business capability.

The following benefits are listed:

### **Reduced “How Do I” tickets**

AVA is intended to reduce support demand for routine, repeatable process questions. This means fewer basic tickets and less time spent by support or project teams answering standard navigation queries.

### **Improved user consistency**

Because AVA is tied to approved procedure guides, it helps standardise guidance across the organisation. Users are more likely to receive the same answer, based on the same source, rather than relying on variable person-to-person advice.

### **Faster query resolution**

Instead of waiting for a response via ticket or manually searching documents, users can get immediate guidance. This shortens resolution cycles and supports operational continuity.

### **Frees up team capacity**

By absorbing routine navigation questions, AVA allows support, enablement, or SME teams to focus on genuine exceptions and more complex work rather than repetitive guidance.

### **Scalable governance layer**

AVA is described as an AI agent that respects program controls. This is perhaps the most strategically important phrasing on the slide. It suggests the tool is not just scalable because it is AI, but scalable because it operates within governance rules rather than outside them.

### **Overall value statement**

The concluding line — **“Turn 15-minute confusion into 15-second clarity”** — acts as the headline business outcome. It conveys productivity, reduced friction, and improved confidence in one phrase.

For a client handover, this slide provides the clearest articulation of the **operational ROI and strategic rationale** behind the tool. It frames AVA as a low-friction, scalable, governed support layer that can improve the user experience while protecting documentation integrity.

## **9. Feedback and issue management**

The final content slide includes two clear post-launch or pilot support mechanisms:

### **Share feedback**

Users are directed to complete the **AVA Pilot Feedback Form** so the team can better understand their experience and improve AVA over time.

### **Report an issue**

Users are also directed to complete the **AVA Issue Report Form** so that problems can be investigated and the AVA experience can continue to improve.

This is significant because it shows that AVA is not being treated as a static deliverable. Instead, it is being introduced with:

- a feedback loop

- an issue management process

- a pilot mindset

- an expectation of iteration and continuous improvement

For client handover, this reinforces that AVA is supported by an operational governance process, not just a technical deployment. It also shows that adoption, user feedback, and issue resolution are part of the solution design.

# **What the deck communicates overall**

Taken as a whole, the document presents AVA as a **controlled, trustworthy, and highly practical AI assistant for enterprise documentation retrieval**.

It communicates that AVA is designed to solve a very specific business problem:

- users struggle to find the right D365 procedure guidance quickly

- support teams receive repeatable navigation questions

- inconsistency arises when users rely on memory or informal advice

- documentation exists, but access is inefficient

AVA’s response to this problem is to provide:

- fast, chat-based access to approved content

- citations and traceability

- source-linked procedural guidance

- controlled out-of-scope handling

- an embedded experience within the existing D365/SharePoint documentation environment

The deck does **not** position AVA as a broad AI transformation platform. Instead, it deliberately keeps the scope focused and credible. That makes the solution easier to trust, easier to govern, and easier to adopt.
