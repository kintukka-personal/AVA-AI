# **Draft answers for WiseTech AI Awards submission**

## **Section 1 — The problem AVA solves**

**1. What was the exact problem that led you to create AVA?**  
AVA was created to solve a persistent navigation problem in D365: users often knew the answer existed in documentation, but could not find the correct Procedure Guide, topic, or steps quickly enough in the moment of need. This created avoidable delays, repeat “How do I…” queries, and unnecessary dependence on SMEs and BAU support.

**2. Before AVA, how were users finding D365 procedures?**  
Before AVA, users primarily relied on manual searching through Procedure Guides in the Atlas Training Hub, asking colleagues, or raising support queries when they could not locate the correct documented process.

**3. What was slow, repetitive, risky, or inefficient about the old way?**  
The old approach was slow because users had to manually search through multiple guides and topics to find the right answer. It was repetitive because the same navigation-based questions were asked repeatedly of SMEs and support teams. It was risky because users could follow the wrong procedure, use the wrong document, or rely on informal guidance instead of approved documentation.

**4. Who was most affected by this problem?**  
The most affected groups were end users performing D365 tasks, SMEs repeatedly answering navigation-based questions, and BAU/support teams handling high volumes of low-value “how do I” queries.

**5. Was this problem especially visible during go-live, hypercare, or post-training BAU?**  
Yes. The problem was especially visible during go-live and post-training BAU, when users had completed training but still needed fast, accurate access to procedures while performing real tasks in the system.

**6. What made this problem worth solving now?**  
This problem became worth solving now because D365 rollout scale, documentation volume, and business expectations around AI-enabled efficiency had all increased. AVA presented a way to reduce navigation friction without weakening governance or relying on uncontrolled AI behaviour.

## **Section 2 — What AVA is**

**7. In one sentence, how would you describe AVA?**  
AVA is a governed documentation assistant that helps users quickly retrieve approved D365 procedures in a way that is scalable, consistent, and auditable.

**8. What are AVA’s primary capabilities today?**  
AVA’s primary capability today is documentation retrieval: locating the right Procedure Guide, identifying the relevant topic, extracting the documented steps, citing the exact source, and linking users back to approved content. It also includes structured clarification logic when a user’s question is too broad or ambiguous.

**9. What does AVA deliberately not do?**  
AVA does not provide undocumented advice, guess user intent, infer missing process details, blend steps across guides, bypass workflows, execute transactions, or replace formal training or BAU support.

**10. What makes AVA different from normal search, SharePoint browsing, or asking a colleague?**  
AVA does more than return documents. It classifies the request, identifies the correct procedure topic, extracts the exact documented steps, cites the source and page range, and refuses safely when the process is not documented. This reduces interpretation effort and improves consistency compared with manual searching or informal support.

**11. Is AVA currently focused mainly on D365 Procedure Guides and QRCs only?**  
Yes. AVA is intentionally restricted to approved WiseTech Global D365 Procedure Guides and QRCs.

**12. What is the current maturity level?**  
AVA has completed a two-month pilot and has now moved into **controlled live usage**. The pilot validated AVA’s core strength as a governed documentation assistant that is highly effective at navigating users to approved Procedure Guide content and retrieving the correct documented steps. Since moving into controlled live usage, a small number of pain points have been identified, particularly where users ask questions that sit outside approved Procedure Guide coverage. This has reinforced that AVA is strongest when used for governed documentation retrieval, while also highlighting opportunities to improve how it handles broader, non-documented queries in future iterations.

## **Section 5 — The AI-first element**

**27. What part of AVA is genuinely AI-enabled rather than just static linking?**  
AVA uses AI to interpret natural language questions, classify intent, identify the most relevant procedure sources, distinguish between direct, partial, ambiguous, and no-match scenarios, and structure its response accordingly. That is materially different from static hyperlinks or manual search.

**28. How does AVA use AI to reduce low-value navigation effort?**  
AVA reduces low-value navigation effort by letting users ask questions in natural language and then performing the retrieval and topic-matching work for them, rather than requiring users to manually browse folders, titles, and long documents.

**29. What part still relies on human judgment?**  
Human judgment remains critical in documentation ownership, process approval, system execution, business decision-making, and review of outputs. AVA supports retrieval; it does not replace accountability or professional judgment.

**30. Why is AVA a good example of WiseTech’s “AI-first, human oversight” mindset?**  
AVA applies AI from the start of the workflow to remove low-value search effort, while preserving human judgment where it matters: users still execute the process, follow controls, validate context, and remain accountable for outcomes. This aligns strongly with WiseTech’s stated expectation that AI supports work rather than replaces judgment.

**31. What work does AVA do from the start that previously sat with humans?**  
AVA takes on the first-line navigation task that previously sat with end users, trainers, SMEs, or support staff: finding the right guide, locating the right topic, and retrieving the relevant documented steps.

## **Section 6 — Human oversight and risk management**

**32. What are the main guardrails you built into AVA?**  
Key guardrails include:

- document-only retrieval

- mandatory citation of approved sources

- exact Procedure Guide/topic/page references

- refusal when content is not documented

- strict gating before step extraction

- no inference or blending across topics

- no workflow bypass or transaction execution.

**33. How does AVA prevent hallucination or unsupported advice?**  
AVA is explicitly instructed not to infer, guess, or interpret beyond documentation. It can only retrieve from approved Procedure Guides and QRCs, and it must refuse when it cannot confidently map the user request to a documented source.

**34. How does AVA handle ambiguous questions?**  
AVA classifies ambiguous questions into structured cases. If multiple topics or guides could apply, it presents a comparison or clarification table and requires user confirmation before extracting steps.

**35. How does AVA handle out-of-scope questions?**  
AVA responds with a verbatim out-of-scope message and directs the user to the Atlas D365 Support Hub or People Leader rather than inventing an answer.

**36. How does AVA make source traceability visible?**  
Every step-based answer must include the exact Procedure Guide name, topic name, and page range before presenting the documented Action/Description table.

**37. What happens when AVA is not confident?**  
When confidence is insufficient, AVA does not extract steps. It asks for clarification or redirects to support, depending on the case.

**38. What controls ensure users still follow approved processes rather than treating AVA as authority on its own?**  
AVA always links back to the originating approved document, refuses undocumented guidance, and does not bypass system controls or approvals. It is positioned as a navigation layer, not a decision-maker.

**39. How have you made human review and accountability visible in the design?**  
AVA’s design keeps humans accountable by limiting AI to retrieval and clarification. Documentation owners remain responsible for source quality, and users remain responsible for applying the correct process and following controls.

## **Section 7 — Responsible use and alignment to WiseTech AI principles**

**40. How does AVA align to accountability?**  
AVA has clear scope, documented rules, and visible source traceability. Responsibility for process execution remains with the user, and responsibility for documentation remains with document owners. This aligns with WiseTech’s accountability principle.

**41. How does it align to transparency and explainability?**  
AVA’s outputs are transparent because they cite the exact Procedure Guide, topic, and page range used. Its response logic is explainable through its structured classification model.

**42. How does it align to privacy and security?**  
AVA operates within the existing Microsoft 365 and Copilot enterprise environment, inheriting the security controls, data protections, and contractual safeguards already in place for approved WiseTech use. It is restricted to approved internal documentation created and governed by business process owners, which avoids reliance on unapproved tools or uncontrolled content. AVA does not create a shadow knowledge base, does not execute transactions, and does not expose customer-sensitive workflows. Its role is tightly constrained to information retrieval and guided navigation within a controlled documentation environment.

**43. How does it align to robustness and safety?**  
AVA is designed to fail first and fail safely. It uses a structured refusal model so that when confidence is low, documentation is ambiguous, or no approved source exists, it does not guess, reconstruct, or provide unsupported procedural advice. Ambiguity is handled through case-based clarification before any steps are extracted. Additional rules have also been introduced to reduce platform-related parsing issues and improve response reliability. Ongoing issues are captured through feedback and issue-reporting forms, and those findings are used to strengthen AVA’s logic in future iterations.

**44. How does it avoid harm or bad process advice?**  
AVA avoids harm by refusing to invent process steps, preventing cross-topic blending, and requiring confirmation before returning steps in ambiguous situations.

**45. Does AVA operate only within approved tooling and approved documentation sources?**  
Yes. AVA is designed to work only with approved internal sources and approved AI tooling.

**46. How have you ensured AVA does not bypass approvals, controls, or financial authority?**  
AVA does not execute transactions, approve anything, or change records. It only retrieves documented guidance and preserves existing workflow and approval structures.

**47. How does AVA fit within WiseTech’s acceptable AI use guidance?**  
AVA aligns strongly with WiseTech’s acceptable use guidance for content creation/editing, training/educational purposes, research/information gathering, and non-critical process support, while retaining human review and avoiding prohibited decision-making or unauthorized transactions.

## **Section 8 — Build journey, experimentation, and learning**

**48. What was your first version of AVA like?**  
The first version of AVA was a lighter retrieval concept focused on helping users locate relevant Procedure Guides and navigate to the right documentation more quickly. It could identify likely source documents and direct users toward approved content, but it was not yet as mature in its handling of ambiguity, strict citation formatting, or controlled step extraction. Over time, AVA evolved into a more governed documentation assistant with stronger classification logic, safer clarification flows, and tighter response controls.

**49. What did not work well early on?**  
Early on, AVA’s biggest challenges were around ambiguity and user interaction. While it was effective at identifying likely documentation, it needed stronger rules to safely handle scenarios where multiple Procedure Guides or Topics could apply. Another early issue was that numeric-only clarification responses from users could trigger a Copilot parsing error, which exposed a platform limitation and required prompt refinement. More broadly, the strict guardrails that made AVA safe for documented process retrieval also made it harder to handle questions that fell outside approved Procedure Guide coverage, highlighting the need to keep its primary use case tightly focused.

**50. What were the biggest design or prompting lessons you learned?**  
The biggest lessons were:

- guardrails must be explicit

- ambiguity handling must be deterministic

- citation format must be enforced

- user confirmation wording matters

- platform quirks, such as numeric-only input handling, must be accounted for in prompt design.

**51. What issues did users uncover during testing or pilot?**  
A key issue uncovered during pilot use was that numeric-only replies during clarification flows could trigger a Copilot parsing error. This was surfaced by a user, diagnosed as a platform parsing limitation, and then mitigated through additional AVA guardrails and fallback logic. The pilot also highlighted a broader usability tension: AVA was highly effective when users asked questions that mapped to approved Procedure Guide content, but more difficult to use when users asked broader questions that sat outside documented process coverage. This reinforced the importance of keeping AVA focused on governed documentation retrieval while continuing to improve how it handles unsupported queries safely.

**52. Can you give 2–3 examples of refinements you made because of user feedback?**  
Yes:

- Clarification flows were updated so AVA no longer tells users to reply with numbers only, after numeric-only responses triggered a Copilot parsing issue

- Numeric-only fallback logic was added so AVA can recover more safely if users still respond that way

- Dedicated feedback channels were established, including an AVA pilot feedback form, a secure issues form, and a Teams updates/feedback channel, to capture findings and continuously improve the experience

**53. What is the best example of AVA becoming more resilient through iteration?**  
The strongest example is the numeric parsing issue: a user reported the failure, the root cause was identified as a Copilot parsing limitation, and AVA’s guardrails were updated to reduce exposure and improve resilience without weakening governance.

**54. What limitations still exist today?**  
AVA remains dependent on the quality, structure, and completeness of the underlying documentation. It also inherits some platform limitations from Copilot and cannot responsibly answer questions that are not covered in approved guides.

**55. What parts of AVA are reusable beyond this specific use case?**

- the intent-classification model

- the direct/partial/ambiguous/no-match framework

- strict citation rules

- clarification and safe-refusal patterns

- feedback and issue-management workflows.

**56. Could the same pattern be applied to other modules, teams, or business functions?**  
Yes. The pattern is reusable across any domain with approved documentation and a need for governed retrieval, especially where consistency and auditability matter.

**57. What makes AVA scalable as documentation grows?**  
AVA scales because it reads from approved source content rather than relying on duplicated manual knowledge maintenance. As new guides are published, AVA’s retrieval capability expands with them.

**58. How does the auto-updating knowledge model work in practice?**  
AVA’s knowledge model is designed to scale with the Atlas Training Hub rather than requiring duplicate maintenance. In practice, when a new approved Procedure Guide is added to the Atlas Training Hub, AVA can begin retrieving from that content without separate manual retraining or rewriting of the knowledge base. A live example of this was the addition of the **34-Procurement (Purchase Agreements) PG**. Once that guide was published, AVA successfully retrieved the documented steps for **Create Purchase Agreement**, including the correct Procedure Guide reference, Topic, and page range. This demonstrated that AVA’s retrieval capability expands automatically as approved documentation grows.

**59. Why is AVA low-maintenance compared with manual knowledge-base upkeep?**  
Maintenance stays where it already belongs: with documentation ownership. AVA does not require separate content rewriting for every update.

**60. What would another team at WiseTech be able to reuse from your approach?**  
Another team could reuse the governance model, prompt design pattern, classification logic, clarification flow, safe-refusal structure, update process, issue form, and pilot feedback loop.
